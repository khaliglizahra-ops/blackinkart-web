How you care for a piercing afterwards matters as much as how it was done.

## The first days

- Keep the area away from water for the first **2 days**. For the following week, apply a thick layer of petroleum jelly to both the entry and exit points before any contact with water.
- After contact with water, gently clean off the petroleum jelly with a clean cotton bud.
- **Never** touch the piercing without disinfecting your hands first. Avoid knocks, friction, and sleeping on it.

## The saline protocol

From the day it is done, for **10 days, at least 5 times a day**, apply saline or a piercing solution to the entry and exit points — 3 or 4 sprays.

> **Home-made saline:** Add one level teaspoon of salt to a cup of boiled water and bring it back to the boil. Once cool, transfer it to a clean spray bottle.

Ointments are generally not recommended, because they keep the wound moist and slow healing. The exception is for people who cannot help touching the area frequently: petroleum jelly or tetracycline ointment 3 times a day for 5 days from the day of piercing. Unless there are signs of allergy or infection, we do not recommend antibiotic ointment.

## General rules

- For a first piercing, use titanium or 316L surgical steel of the correct standard — see [piercing materials](/en/piercing/piercing-materials).
- A new piercing should not be moved or removed for the first **2 months**.
- Do not leave a piercing empty for at least **6 months to a year**. Cartilage and mucosal tissue can close very quickly.
- For oral piercings, use an antiseptic mouthwash 3 times a day for a week. With a tongue piercing, stick to cold and liquid food for the first week.

If anything goes wrong, your first step should be to talk to us.
